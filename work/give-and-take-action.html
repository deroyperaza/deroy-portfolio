What if a city game rewarded everyday civics instead of vice?

Give and Take Action

A free game set in a satirical Brooklyn. Instead of looting, you pick up groceries for a community fridge, meet the nonprofits doing the work and give to the one that moves you. Launched with Brooklyn Org for GivingTuesday.

Most open-world video games hand you a city and dare you to wreck it. Give and Take Action flips that: the point is getting to know your neighbors and figuring out how to make your community better. Instead of running from the law, you learn how a community board works, how to start a tenant association and how to win a block party permit.

It opens at Brooklyn Org, Brooklyn's community foundation, where Adaeze, a grants officer, explains what a community foundation does and sends you out to meet four of this year's grantees. Hundreds of Brooklyn nonprofits are in the game, each with a fictional staffer who tells you what the real organization does. Every mission ends with the real groups doing that work and a link to donate on Brooklyn Gives.

The map is real Brooklyn: all 58 neighborhoods and all 156 subway stations. Twenty missions take on real civic needs, and Shirley Chisholm, Jackie Robinson, Walt Whitman and the Notorious B.I.G. wait on their old blocks with a piece of true Brooklyn history.

It started in a group chat about GTA 6, when a friend wrote back, “we should make our own game.” My kids played the prototype and pitched ideas along the way. Free in any browser, on a laptop or a phone.
